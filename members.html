Wongu 圓球 · Aboji Exercise

What Is Wongu?

圓球 · A sphere built around a center

Wongu is the Korean reading used here for 圓球, a concept passed down through Aboji Exercise. It represents the relationship between a stable center, opposing directions, and continuous circular movement. In this program, your Wongu grows visually as you keep returning to practice.

The sphere is a symbolic picture of continuity and participation, not a measurement of health or proof that a workout was completed.

The meaning of the numbers

8

An eight-day rhythm. Try to practice every day when possible, ideally at least twice in each eight-day period, and aim for at least once.

40

One forty-day chapter. A small Wongu is fully completed only when all 40 daily participation days are recorded in that chapter.

120

Three chapters of 40 days. When all three small Wongu are complete, they come together as one Great Wongu.

13

In Aboji Exercise's symbolic interpretation, 13 represents the idea of a center that emerges beyond a completed cycle.

How the form develops

3 · Awakening: three points and a center. 6 · Growth: six opposing directions. 12 · Completion of directions: six original directions plus eight diagonals. Then twelve cube edges, face connections, inward/outward circulation, and synchronization are added one practice at a time. Only the 40th counted practice closes the completed small Wongu shell.

The principle of continuity

According to Aboji's teaching, a single intense session is not the whole story. Returning regularly, finding your center, and repeating simple movements build a rhythm over time. The eight-day cycles are reminders to reconnect, not deadlines to punish yourself.

For your Wongu to grow, regular member-page check-ins and actual exercise practice both matter. On this member site, a verified page visit counts at most once per calendar day toward the digital Wongu. Physical exercise is encouraged alongside that visit, but the check-in alone cannot verify that exercise took place.

Try Aboji Exercise after waking, when possible. A shorter evening practice can also be helpful. On a tiring day, gentle breathing practice, even while lying down, can be a way to maintain the habit.

The numerical meanings and relationship to cosmic cycles are teachings and symbols within Aboji Exercise, not scientific or medical claims. This member page uses verified, once-per-day page visits as participation records. Visits do not verify exercise performance.
